# Cooling Water Systems

Two distinct circuits serve the machinery: a closed low temperature and high temperature fresh water system, and an open seawater system that removes heat from both through the central cooler. Seawater does not enter the engine at any point.

## Jacket cooling water

Jacket cooling water outlet temperature from the main engine is maintained at eighty-five degrees Celsius. The temperature is controlled by a three-way valve mixing hot outlet water back into the inlet, not by varying the seawater flow. Attempting to control jacket temperature at the seawater side will produce a slow oscillation the control system cannot settle.

Outlet temperature is held high deliberately. A cooler engine suffers cold corrosion of the liner surface where sulphuric acid formed during combustion condenses. The eighty-five degree figure is a compromise between that risk and the thermal stress of a hotter jacket.

Where the engine is operating at low load for a prolonged period, the outlet temperature will fall unless the control valve compensates. This is a common finding during slow steaming and is addressed in the main engine instruction.

## Water treatment and testing

The closed fresh water circuit is dosed with a nitrite-based corrosion inhibitor. The nitrite concentration is maintained between 1200 and 2400 parts per million. Below the lower figure the protective film on cast iron surfaces is not maintained and pitting begins. Above the upper figure there is no additional protection and the excess simply increases the cost and the conductivity of the water.

Chloride content of the closed circuit must not exceed 50 parts per million. A rising chloride is the clearest indication of seawater ingress, most often through a central cooler tube or a leaking seal on a pump. Chloride does not decrease on its own; a fall in chloride means the system has been diluted with fresh water, which also dilutes the inhibitor.

Testing is carried out weekly and the results entered in the water treatment log. Note that the auxiliary boiler feed water has an entirely different treatment regime and a different chloride limit, described in the boiler instruction. The two test kits are not interchangeable and using the boiler reagents on jacket water will give a meaningless result.

## Expansion tank

The expansion tank accommodates thermal expansion and provides a static head at the pump suction. It is fitted with a low level alarm. A persistent need to top up indicates a leak; the tank should not be topped up repeatedly without finding the cause, because each addition dilutes the inhibitor and introduces oxygen.

Top-up water is distilled or produced by the fresh water generator. Shore water and potable water are not used, because both carry chloride and hardness that the closed system is not designed to tolerate.

## Central cooler and seawater side

The central cooler is a plate type unit with titanium plates. Seawater flow is maintained continuously whether or not the main engine is running, because the auxiliary engines and the air conditioning plant reject heat to the same circuit.

The seawater side is fitted with a marine growth prevention system at the sea chests. Fouling of the seawater side presents as a rising fresh water temperature that the three-way valve cannot correct even when fully open to the cooler. Plate cleaning is carried out at the planned interval, and the differential pressure across the cooler is logged each watch as the early indication.
