# Ballast Water Management

Ballast water is carried to maintain draught, trim, stability and hull stress within permitted limits. Because it is taken from one port and discharged at another, it carries organisms between ecosystems, and its management is regulated accordingly.

## The D-2 discharge standard

Ballast water discharged from the vessel must meet the D-2 standard. Discharged water must contain fewer than ten viable organisms per cubic metre that are fifty micrometres or greater in minimum dimension, and fewer than ten viable organisms per millilitre that are between ten and fifty micrometres.

The standard also sets limits on indicator microbes, including toxicogenic vibrio cholerae, escherichia coli and intestinal enterococci. These are expressed per one hundred millilitres and are not the same units as the organism counts above.

Compliance is achieved through the ballast water treatment system rather than by exchange. Exchange is retained only as a contingency where the treatment system is inoperative and the alternative arrangements have been agreed.

## The treatment system

The treatment system uses filtration followed by ultraviolet irradiation. Filtration removes larger organisms and sediment on uptake; the ultraviolet stage inactivates what remains. Treatment is applied on uptake and again on discharge, because organisms can regrow in the tank during a long voyage.

The ultraviolet lamps have a rated life and are replaced at the planned interval regardless of whether they still illuminate; output falls well before failure and the system cannot detect a lamp that is lit but weak. The filter is backflushed automatically on differential pressure, and the backflush is returned to the sea at the point of uptake, never carried to another location.

Uptake is avoided in shallow water, in areas of known algal bloom, near dredging operations, and where the vessel is aground or where sediment is being disturbed. Sediment taken into a ballast tank is not removed by any treatment and accumulates until the next tank cleaning.

## Contingency exchange

Where exchange is used, it is carried out at least two hundred nautical miles from the nearest land and in water at least two hundred metres deep. Where that is not possible, exchange is carried out as far from land as possible and in any case at least fifty nautical miles from the nearest land and in water at least two hundred metres deep.

Exchange is not carried out where it would take the vessel outside its permitted stability, strength or bridge visibility limits. The master's judgement on that point is final and is recorded.

## The Ballast Water Record Book

Every uptake, treatment, internal transfer, discharge and accidental release is recorded in the Ballast Water Record Book. Entries are signed by the officer in charge, and each completed page is signed by the master.

The Ballast Water Record Book is retained on board for two years from the date of the last entry, and thereafter under the company's control for a further three years.

This retention period differs from that of the Oil Record Book, which is covered in the bilge and oily water instruction. Confusing the two books, or the two retention periods, is a common finding at inspection.

## Tank cleaning and sediment

Sediment accumulating in ballast tanks is removed during scheduled tank inspection, and the sediment is landed to a reception facility. Sediment is not flushed overboard, because it carries exactly the organisms the treatment system exists to control.
