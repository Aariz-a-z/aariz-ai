# Main Engine Operation

The main propulsion engine is a two-stroke crosshead diesel driving a fixed-pitch propeller through a direct-coupled shaft line. It is designed for continuous operation on heavy fuel oil, with distillate fuel used only for the manoeuvring condition described below and for entry into emission control areas.

## Load range and slow steaming

Continuous operation below fifteen per cent of maximum continuous rating is not permitted without the low-load package fitted. Below that figure the scavenge air pressure is insufficient to keep combustion clean, and the result is fouling of the exhaust gas ways, deposits on the piston crown and a rising exhaust temperature that the engineer will misread as an overload. Where prolonged slow steaming is planned, the auxiliary blowers must be left in automatic and the cylinder oil feed rate reviewed against the guidance in the lubricating oil instructions.

Scavenge air pressure at full load is 2.4 bar. A pressure materially below that at a given load indicates either a fouled air cooler, a turbocharger fault, or a leaking scavenge non-return valve. Do not attempt to compensate by increasing fuel index; that raises thermal load on components that are already receiving insufficient air.

## Critical speed range

The shaft line has a first-order torsional resonance between forty-two and fifty-eight revolutions per minute. This band is marked on the bridge and engine room telegraph repeaters. The engine must be accelerated through the band without dwelling in it, and continuous running inside the band is prohibited under any circumstance, including during manoeuvring, trials and astern running. Sustained operation within the resonance band produces torsional stress in the intermediate shaft that no routine inspection will reveal until a crack has propagated.

## Exhaust gas temperature monitoring

Each cylinder's exhaust outlet temperature is recorded at every watch. The permissible deviation of any single cylinder from the mean of all cylinders is fifty degrees Celsius. A cylinder reading persistently high relative to the mean indicates a fuel injection fault, a leaking exhaust valve, or an incorrectly set fuel pump index. A cylinder reading persistently low may indicate a seized fuel pump plunger or an injector that has ceased to atomise.

The turbocharger inlet temperature is a separate measurement and is not subject to the fifty-degree deviation rule, because it is a mixed temperature drawn from all cylinders and has no per-cylinder mean to deviate from.

## Turbocharger surge

Surge presents as a repeated loud report from the turbocharger air intake, accompanied by a fluctuating scavenge pressure. The usual causes are a heavily fouled turbine side, a sudden load reduction, or heavy weather causing the propeller to race and the engine governor to hunt. Reduce load immediately and investigate. Persistent surging damages the compressor and the thrust bearing of the turbocharger rotor.

Water washing of the turbine side is carried out at the interval given in the planned maintenance instructions and is not a response to surge. Washing a surging machine at load will not clear the condition and may crack the nozzle ring through thermal shock.

## Starting air

The starting air receivers are charged to thirty bar. Two receivers are provided and both are to be kept charged at sea. The engine is required to be capable of twelve consecutive starts without recharging when the shaft is direct-coupled to a fixed-pitch propeller. Starting air lines are fitted with a bursting disc and a flame arrester at each cylinder start valve, both of which are inspected at the planned interval.

## Interfaces with other systems

Jacket cooling water outlet temperature, lubricating oil inlet pressure and fuel oil viscosity at the injection pump are the three parameters most often mistaken for main engine faults when the cause lies in the supporting system. Each is covered in its own instruction: cooling water, lubricating oil, and fuel oil treatment respectively. Before opening a unit on the main engine, confirm that the supporting system is delivering to specification.
