# Machinery Space Emergency Procedures

This instruction covers fire, flooding, blackout and loss of propulsion in the machinery space. It takes precedence over any routine operating instruction where the two conflict.

## Blackout and the emergency generator

On loss of main electrical power the emergency generator starts automatically and connects to the emergency switchboard within forty-five seconds. The emergency switchboard supplies steering gear, emergency lighting, navigation lights, communications, the fire pump and the watertight door indication.

Recovery from blackout follows a set order: confirm the emergency generator has connected, establish the cause, start an auxiliary engine on the main switchboard, restore essential services, then restore propulsion auxiliaries before attempting to start the main engine. Attempting to restore the main engine before the supporting pumps are running will produce a second blackout on the starting load.

Do not reset a tripped breaker without knowing why it tripped. A breaker that trips a second time on a fault it was protecting against will not necessarily trip a third time before the fault becomes a fire.

## Fire in the machinery space

On discovering fire, raise the alarm first. Attack the fire with portable means only if it is small and only if doing so does not delay the alarm.

Where the fire cannot be controlled, the space is prepared for fixed installation release: all personnel are accounted for and evacuated, ventilation fans are stopped, ventilation flaps and skylights are closed, quick-closing valves on the fuel and lubricating oil tanks are operated from outside the space, and fuel pumps and purifiers are stopped from their remote stops.

The quick-closing valves are tested at the planned interval. They are frequently found seized because they are tested by operation of the release and not by confirming the valve has actually closed.

Release of the fixed fire extinguishing medium requires two deliberate actions at the release station and is authorised by the master. Once released, the space is not re-entered until it has been confirmed cool and has been ventilated, and re-entry is made with breathing apparatus regardless of how long the space has been closed.

## Flooding

On detecting flooding, identify the source before selecting the pumping arrangement. The bilge system is not sized to overcome a significant hull or seawater system breach, and pumping a rising level without isolating the source only delays the decision to close the compartment.

Seawater system leaks are isolated at the sea chest suction and discharge valves. The position of every ship side valve and its local and remote operating positions form part of the familiarisation of every engineer joining the vessel.

## Loss of propulsion

On loss of propulsion the bridge is informed immediately and without waiting to establish the cause. The bridge requires that information to manage the navigational situation, and a delay of even a minute may remove options that were available.

Where the main engine has shut down on a protective trip, the trip is identified before any attempt to restart. Overriding a protective shutdown to regain propulsion is a decision for the chief engineer and the master jointly, taken only where the navigational hazard exceeds the machinery risk, and it is recorded.

## Records

Every emergency, every use of a fixed installation, and every override of a protective device is recorded in the engine log with times. Where oil has entered the bilge or the sea, the recording requirements of the bilge and oily water instruction also apply.
