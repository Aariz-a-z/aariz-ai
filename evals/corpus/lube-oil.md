# Lubricating Oil System

The main engine system oil serves the bearings, the crosshead, the camshaft drive and the piston cooling circuit. Cylinder lubrication is a separate once-through system using a different oil and is described in its own section below.

## System oil pressure

Lubricating oil inlet pressure at the engine is 4.5 bar under normal conditions. A low pressure alarm is set at 2.8 bar. Automatic shutdown of the main engine occurs at 2.2 bar. These three figures are distinct and must not be conflated: the alarm gives the engineer an opportunity to intervene, and the shutdown protects the bearings when that intervention has not succeeded.

The shutdown may be overridden only from the engine control room and only when the vessel is in a situation where loss of propulsion presents a greater hazard than bearing damage. Any override is recorded with the time and the reason.

A falling pressure with normal pump discharge indicates a leak, a blocked filter differential, or a bearing clearance that has opened. Confirm the reading at a second gauge before acting; a faulty transmitter has caused more unnecessary shutdowns than genuine loss of pressure.

## The lubricating oil purifier

The lubricating oil purifier operates at ninety degrees Celsius. It runs continuously in bypass from the sump, drawing from the lowest point and returning to the far end so that the whole charge circulates. The purifier is not a filter and does not remove insoluble combustion products; those are removed by the automatic backflush filter upstream of the engine.

The fuel oil purifier operates at a higher temperature and is covered in the fuel oil instruction. Setting the lubricating oil purifier to the fuel oil temperature will accelerate oxidation of the system oil and strip the additive package. The machines look alike and the control panels are the same model, so confirm the label before adjusting a set point.

## Water content

Water content of the system oil must not exceed 0.2 per cent. Water enters from the piston cooling space, from a leaking cooler tube, or from condensation in a cold sump. A rising water content with no visible ingress usually indicates the cooler; isolate and pressure test it rather than assuming condensation.

Free water is removed by the purifier. Emulsified water is not, and its presence indicates that the additive package has begun to break down. A sample is landed for laboratory analysis at the planned interval and whenever water content rises without explanation.

## Cylinder lubrication

Cylinder oil is injected directly onto the liner surface through quills and is consumed. It is not recovered. The feed rate is set to 0.8 grams per kilowatt hour at normal service load. The rate is adjusted for the sulphur content of the fuel in use, because the alkaline reserve of the cylinder oil neutralises the acids formed during combustion.

Running a low base number cylinder oil on high sulphur fuel causes corrosive wear of the liner. Running a high base number oil on low sulphur fuel leaves unreacted alkaline additive as deposit on the piston crown and in the ring grooves. Neither error is visible from the control room, and both are found at the next scavenge port inspection.

Following a fuel changeover for emission control area entry, the cylinder oil grade is reviewed. The changeover procedure itself is in the fuel oil instruction.

## Sampling

System oil samples are drawn from a running system at a point after the pump and before the filter, never from the sump drain. A sample drawn from a static system or from the lowest point will contain settled water and sediment and will not represent the oil the engine is receiving.
