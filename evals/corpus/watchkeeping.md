# Engine Room Watchkeeping

The machinery space is certified for periodically unattended operation. This instruction covers both the unattended condition and the manned watch, and the transition between them.

## Unattended machinery space operation

When the space is unattended, one engineer is nominated as duty engineer and carries the alarm transfer unit. The duty engineer must be able to reach the engine control room within ten minutes of an alarm being raised, and must remain on board and fit for duty throughout the period.

The space may not be left unattended if any of the following applies: a machinery alarm is standing unacknowledged, the bilge level in any space is above normal, a fire detection zone is isolated, or the vessel is manoeuvring, in restricted visibility, or in confined waters.

Before leaving the space unattended the duty engineer records the machinery status, confirms all standby pumps are in automatic, tests the alarm transfer, and confirms that the bridge has acknowledged the transfer of responsibility.

## The dead man alarm

While the space is unattended and an engineer is working within it, the personnel alarm system is activated. The engineer must reset the system at intervals not exceeding thirty minutes. Failure to reset raises a pre-alarm in the space and, if still unanswered, a general alarm on the bridge and in the accommodation.

The thirty minute period is not a response time; it is the maximum interval between resets. An engineer entering the space for a task expected to take five minutes still activates the system.

## Manned watch rounds

In the manned condition, the watchkeeper carries out rounds of the machinery spaces at intervals not exceeding two hours. Rounds cover the main engine, the auxiliary engines, the purifier room, the steering gear compartment and the shaft tunnel. Readings are entered in the log at the time they are taken, not reconstructed at the end of the watch.

The two-hour rounds interval and the thirty-minute dead man reset interval are separate requirements arising from different conditions and are often confused. Rounds apply to the manned watch; the dead man alarm applies to a person working in an otherwise unattended space.

## Handover

The relieving engineer arrives fifteen minutes before the hour to allow a handover walk round. The outgoing engineer does not leave until the relief has confirmed understanding of the machinery status, any standing defects, any work in progress and any equipment isolated or locked off.

Handover is not completed by conversation alone. The relief physically observes the main parameters and confirms the position of any valve or breaker that has been changed during the preceding watch.

## Response to an alarm

On receiving an alarm, the duty engineer proceeds to the engine control room, identifies the alarm, and acknowledges it only after understanding what it indicates. Acknowledging an alarm to silence it before identifying it is the single most common contributor to an escalating machinery casualty.

Where the alarm indicates a condition covered by the emergency instruction, that instruction takes precedence over any routine procedure in this document.
