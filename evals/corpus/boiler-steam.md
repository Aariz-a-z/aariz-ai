# Auxiliary Boiler and Steam System

The auxiliary boiler supplies steam for fuel oil heating, tank heating, accommodation services and the fresh water generator. At sea the exhaust gas economiser carries most of the load and the oil-fired boiler operates intermittently.

## Operating pressure

The boiler working pressure is 7 bar. The safety valves are set to lift at 8.4 bar, which is twenty per cent above working pressure. Two safety valves are fitted and both are set to the same pressure.

The safety valves are tested by raising steam and allowing the valves to lift under pressure, which is carried out annually. They are not to be tested by easing gear alone; easing gear confirms that the valve is free but proves nothing about the set pressure.

Do not confuse the boiler working pressure with the starting air pressure or the lubricating oil pressure quoted in other instructions. All three are expressed in bar and all three appear on gauges in the same compartment.

## Feed water treatment

Boiler feed water is treated to control alkalinity, oxygen and hardness. The pH of the boiler water is maintained between 9.5 and 11. Chloride content of the boiler water must not exceed 100 parts per million.

This chloride limit applies to the boiler and is not the same as the limit for the closed jacket cooling circuit, which is lower and is given in the cooling water instruction. The two systems use different reagents and different test procedures.

A rising chloride in boiler water indicates seawater contamination, most commonly through a condenser leak or through contaminated make-up. Where chloride rises, the boiler is blown down and the source found before it is refilled.

## The cascade tank

Condensate returns to the cascade tank, where it is observed for oil contamination before being returned to the boiler. Oil in the cascade tank is the most serious contamination the boiler can suffer: a film of oil on a heating surface prevents heat transfer to the water and the surface overheats locally.

Oil reaches the cascade tank through a leaking heating coil in a fuel or sludge tank. Where oil is found, the affected coil is isolated by pressure testing each in turn and the tank contents are dumped, not returned.

## Fuel for the boiler

In port and during manoeuvring the boiler burns marine gas oil. At sea it may burn heavy fuel oil drawn from the same service tank as the main engine, subject to the sulphur limits described in the fuel oil instruction, which apply to the boiler exactly as they apply to the main engine.

Burner atomisation is checked at each lighting up. A poorly atomising burner produces carbon deposits in the furnace and on the tube surfaces, and unburnt oil accumulating in the furnace presents a real risk of a furnace blowback at the next ignition attempt.

## Water level

The gauge glasses are blown through at the start of every watch to confirm they read true. A gauge glass that has not been blown through may show a level that no longer exists. Low water level is the most dangerous condition the boiler can reach, and the low level cut-out is tested at the planned interval rather than relied upon without proof.
