# Bilge and Oily Water Separator

Machinery space bilges collect leakage, condensate and washing water, all of which may contain oil. This instruction covers the treatment of that mixture, the limits on its discharge, and the records that must be kept.

## The fifteen parts per million limit

Oily mixture from machinery space bilges may be discharged to sea only through approved filtering equipment and only when the oil content of the effluent does not exceed 15 parts per million. The equipment is fitted with a monitor and an automatic stopping device that returns the effluent to the bilge holding tank whenever the limit is exceeded.

The stopping device is not a nuisance to be defeated. Any arrangement that allows effluent to bypass the monitor, whether a fitted line, a flexible hose or a portable pump, is an illegal discharge arrangement regardless of whether it is used.

Discharge is additionally prohibited entirely within special areas and within any area where local regulation forbids it, irrespective of the oil content achieved. The fifteen parts per million figure is a ceiling, not a permission.

## Operation of the separator

The separator is run with a steady suction and a full inlet. Running it on a suction that draws air, or starting it with the vessel rolling heavily so that the bilge suction is alternately covered and uncovered, will produce an emulsion that the equipment cannot separate and the monitor will alarm.

Detergents and cleaning chemicals in the bilge are the most common cause of a separator that will not achieve the limit. A bilge that has received deck cleaner or degreaser should be pumped to the holding tank and landed ashore rather than processed.

## Sludge

Sludge from the fuel and lubricating oil purifiers, from settling tank draining and from separator discharge is collected in the sludge tank. Sludge is not discharged to sea under any circumstance. It is landed to a reception facility or, where an incinerator is fitted and permitted, burned on board.

The sludge tank level is recorded and the tank is not permitted to reach a level at which routine purifier operation must be suspended.

## The Oil Record Book

All transfers, discharges and disposals of oil and oily mixtures are recorded in the Oil Record Book Part I without delay. Each entry is signed by the officer in charge of the operation, and each completed page is signed by the master.

Entries are made at the time of the operation. A book written up at the end of a voyage from memory or from rough notes is a false record, and is treated as such by an inspector regardless of whether the underlying operations were lawful.

The Oil Record Book is retained on board for three years from the date of the last entry.

Note that ballast water operations are recorded in a separate book with its own retention requirement, described in the ballast water instruction. The two books are not interchangeable and an entry made in the wrong book is a deficiency in both.

## Bilge alarms

High level alarms are fitted in each bilge well. An alarm that is standing prevents the machinery space being left unattended, as stated in the watchkeeping instruction. The alarm is not silenced and left; the level is investigated and the cause found.
